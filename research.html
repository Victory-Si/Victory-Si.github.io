Research

My work lies at the intersection of wireless localization, statistical signal processing, and learning-based sensing.

A central goal is to enable robust spatial inference when sensing conditions, available labels, and wireless environments change.

01

Cross-scenario localization

Developing transferable wireless representations and localization models that remain robust across changing buildings, devices, and environments.

Domain generalization · Foundation models · Fingerprint positioning
02

Cooperative & crowdsourced positioning

Learning from unlabeled trajectories and interactions among agents to reduce reliance on dense manual calibration and labeled reference points.

Crowdsourcing · Multi-agent learning · Reinforcement learning
03

Wireless and array sensing

Studying direction-of-arrival estimation and performance limits using continuous and tri-polarized aperture arrays, with applications to wireless sensing.

Array processing · DOA / CRLB · Integrated sensing
Current research question. What wireless knowledge can transfer between previously unseen scenarios, and what information must be learned from the new environment?

Selected research topics

Indoor positioning and radio fingerprinting; learning from crowdsourced trajectories; robust localization under domain shift; multi-agent interaction; continuous-aperture array sensing; direction-of-arrival estimation and information-theoretic performance bounds.

Selected work is listed on the Publications page, including IEEE Transactions on Wireless Communications, IEEE Transactions on Communications, IEEE Transactions on Vehicular Technology, and IEEE Wireless Communications.
